Happy Pouch Help · Statistics

How to read the statistics

The “Statistics” screen answers four questions: how much went out and came in, whether that is more or less than usual, what it was spent on, and what is still ahead. Everything is calculated on your phone and never leaves it.

Period

There is one period for the whole screen, written at the top: “November 2026”. The arrows next to it step it back and forward. The length of the period (day, week, month, quarter, year) is chosen in the side menu. You cannot move into the future.

Three figures: expenses, income, balance

The balance is income minus expenses. Under each figure is the change against the last period.

  • The sign shows the direction: “+10%” is more, “−4%” is less.
  • The thumb and the color show whether that is good or bad. Thumbs-up, green: better, because expenses fell or income grew. Thumbs-down, red: worse.

Until the period ends, it is compared with the same number of days of the previous one: 12 days of November with the first 12 days of October. The caption says so: “vs. last month, same 12 days”.

Spending pace

Two lines: the bright one is this period's spending from its start, day by day; the pale one is the same for the last period. Bright above pale means you are spending faster than last time; below, slower.

Dynamics

The columns are neighboring periods of the same length; the chosen one is highlighted. Above the columns is a switch: expenses, income, balance, Joys (the share of little joys in spending). Tap any column and the whole screen moves to that period.

What changed

A few plain-language lines: where you saved the most or spent the most compared with the last period, how the balance came out, what share went to little joys, how spending compares with the same period a year ago, and how many days had no spending.

The block appears when there are enough records to compare: about a month of tracking.

Still ahead this month

What usually still comes before the end of the month:

  • Payments you have set up, with the date and amount from the “Payments” tab.
  • Bills the app found itself: categories that are paid every month, such as utilities, phone, subscriptions, rent, tuition. Next to each is the day you usually pay and how much. If part is already paid, the remainder is shown.
  • Everyday essentials: how much usually still goes to groceries, fuel and other essentials before the end of the month.
  • Total ahead: the sum of everything listed.

Below is the forecast of the balance at the end of the month. It does not equal “on hand minus total ahead”: it also includes your usual income for that time.

The amounts come from the last six months. With less than three months of tracking, bills are listed without an amount: “amount not known yet”.

Where the money goes

One bar split into kinds of spending, with the share of each:

KindWhat is in it
Essentialsgroceries, housing, utilities, phone, transport, subscriptions, loans, tuition
Hobbies and sporthobbies, gym, clubs, courses, books
Little joyscafés, treats, taxis, gifts
Unexpectedrepairs, medical care, fines
Everything elsecategories with no kind assigned

The app assigns the kind itself from the category name. You can change it in the category card: “Settings” → the category → “What kind of expense is this”.

Little joys

Little joys reflect your quality of life: small, inexpensive pleasures such as treats for yourself, gifts, cafés, taxis. A rough guide to their share of spending:

  • under 5%: low: almost everything goes to essentials;
  • 5 to 20%: normal;
  • over 20%: high.

If little joys for the period exceed income, the app flags that in a separate line.

Categories

The list of the period's categories: amount, share of spending and change against the last period. The “Expenses / Income” switch is at the top of the list. Tap a category to open its history by month, its subcategories and the reason for the change: more purchases, or a larger amount each time.

All time

These lines do not depend on the chosen period:

  • Personal inflation: how much the prices of the things you buy regularly went up over the year.
  • The most and the least expensive days of the week.
  • Records: the month and the day with the highest spending.
  • A large expense: a one-off purchase far above the usual. It is listed separately and left out of records and habits so that it does not skew them.

Your standing relative to the region

Your income and spending over 30 days compared with the average wage and the living minimum. You enter the figures yourself in “Settings”, section “My region”: your national statistics office publishes them. If you enter nothing, the block is not shown.
