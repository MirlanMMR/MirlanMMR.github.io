Calculator: Themes & Widget · Android · free

A calculator with five screens that you build yourself

How it all started

I wanted to download a calculator with a comfortable layout. That's all. I searched for a long time, downloaded a couple of dozen, and none of them worked for me. First came surprise, then fatigue, then genuine disappointment. At some point I waved it off and used the one that came preinstalled.

But nothing lasts forever under the moon. The age of AI arrived. I didn't jump in right away: like many of you, I spent a couple of years watching reels about neural networks and generated videos. Then one day I tried making an app with AI.

The first thing I made wasn't a calculator but a counting app for kids: a ruler with a slider. You put the slider on 2, and to add 2 more you take two steps together with the child, and it lands on 4. I think it's a real leap for a child: they stop counting on their fingers and see right away how the result comes about. I'm convinced of that, though it's my opinion, not a scientific study. I'll tell that story separately. That's where my first steps in vibe coding began.

So, back to the calculator. I didn't want to make one more just like the rest. I needed a calculator you can customize as far as possible. But that's not the main thing. The main thing is that it has not one or two screens but five. Why not? A PC can have three monitors. I could have gone higher, but I decided five was enough.

Why so many screens? So you don't have to keep the result in your head. Swipe the screen aside, calculate on a new one, and if you need to look something up, swipe back to the first. In other words, it's a rethink of history and memory in a convenient format.

In short. A calculator for Android. You can move, remove and add buttons, and pick the theme, color, font and sounds. The main difference is that it has five screens: calculate on one, swipe aside and carry on with another. There is a loan calculator, percentages, geometry, a converter and a home-screen widget. It works offline. It is free, with a banner ad at the bottom. The interface is in Russian and English.

What's in it

Build it your way.

  • 22 themes: light, dark and "electronic" ones with glowing seven-segment digits like old calculators (red and green LED, red and green LCD).
  • Your accent color, 5 button shapes, 7 fonts.
  • Your own layout: move, remove and add buttons. Put + − × ÷ on the right or the left, which is handy for left-handed users.
  • Tap effects (ripple, rings, shine, sparks), key sounds and vibration. All optional.

Calculate.

  • 5 screens: calculate on one, swipe aside and carry on with another. Up to 10 favorite calculators, switch between them on the fly.
  • An engineering menu on the fx button: roots, powers, trigonometry, logarithms, π and e.
  • Calculation history, cursor editing, copy and paste.

Formulas at hand.

  • Percent: what percent a number is, add or subtract a percent, percent change, tax, compound growth.
  • Geometry: circle, rectangle, triangle, box, cylinder, sphere, cone. Enter the values and get the answer.
  • Converter: length, mass, temperature, area, volume, speed, data.

Loans. Annuity and differentiated payments, a payment schedule, fees and the real annual rate. You can check a flat-rate offer and see what it really costs.

Widget. A mini calculator on your home screen that stretches to full screen. Opacity and theme are adjustable. Examples solved in the widget go to the history.

What it doesn't have

  • For now the interface is only in Russian and English. It follows your phone language, and on Android 13 and up you can set the language for the app separately.
  • A banner ad from Google AdMob is shown at the bottom. There are no paid features or subscriptions.
  • The loan and percent tools give estimates, not financial advice. Check your contract for exact terms.
  • It needs Android 8.0 or newer.
