2+2 | Math for Kids · Android · for ages 4–7 · free

Math for kids: counting with a little train

How it all started

It started with a simple observation. Children learn from zero, and at first they all count on their fingers. I wanted to give them something they can see. A number line, for instance: the numbers stand in a row, and any operation is steps along it.

How does it work? There is a ruler from 0 to 10. We put the slider on 2. To add 2 more, we take two steps together with the child, and the slider lands on 4. There is almost nothing to explain, because it is all plain to see.

I think this is a leap for a child: from fingers straight to the logic of calculation. I'm convinced of it, though it is my opinion, not a scientific study.

Coming up with such a trainer is easy, but building it is much harder. It was my first app, and my first steps in vibe coding began with it: I made it together with AI.

Over time we added a lot. Besides numbers up to 10 there are now tens and hundreds (up to 1000), negative numbers and decimal fractions. And we turned the ruler into a railway. That is why a little train is on the cover, and now it is the train, not a slider, that steps along the numbers. We added different backgrounds and music to keep children interested. And for correctly solved examples there is a reward: a new vehicle unlocks and confetti rains down.

I also wanted the app to feel like a diary, not a textbook. So it has a welcome screen where the child picks a nickname and a future profession. From then on it is their own personal app, under their own name.

We will be happy if even one child learns to count with the help of our app!

In short. An Android app that teaches kids aged 4–7 to add and subtract. A little train steps along a number line while the child counts the steps. You can start with numbers up to 10 and move on to tens and hundreds (up to 1000), then try negative numbers and decimal fractions. Solved examples unlock new vehicles. There is no sign-up and no in-app purchases. The app is free but has ads that are requested as child-directed. The interface is in 42 languages and follows your phone language.

What's in it

  • A personal app. In the welcome screen the child picks a nickname and a future profession. All of it is stored only on the device.
  • Learning you can see. Move the train and count the steps. When you see how it happens, the logic of arithmetic clicks much faster.
  • From simple to harder. Numbers up to 10, then tens (up to 100) and hundreds (up to 1000). For those who go further: negative numbers and decimal fractions, introduced through simple play.
  • Rewards. Solve a few examples and a new vehicle opens: a car, a tractor, a ship, an airplane, a flying saucer, a yellow submarine, a hot air balloon. Each has its own sound and leaves smoke or bubbles behind. A correct answer sets off confetti.
  • Vehicles unlock one by one. First after 5 solved examples, then 3, then 1. After that every vehicle is open for 18 hours.
  • Backgrounds. Drawn like a child's pencil picture: sky, house, notebook, space, sea, winter and a flowery meadow. Swipe to change them.
  • Sound. Gentle background music that one tap turns off, and five step sounds to choose from.
  • Comfortable. A dark theme for study at any time of day. Works on phones and tablets, in portrait and landscape.

What it doesn't have

  • The app teaches addition and subtraction. There is no multiplication or division.
  • There are ads from Google AdMob. They are requested as child-directed, with a "G" rating, and personalized ads are not used. There are no paid features or in-app purchases.
  • The app collects anonymous technical statistics (Firebase Analytics): app opens, app version, device model. It does not collect names, email or other personal data. Details are in the privacy policy.
  • It needs Android 8.0 or newer.
